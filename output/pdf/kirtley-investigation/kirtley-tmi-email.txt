Subject: Kirtley / Thompson (1955–58): discrepant entries, reused starred numbers and “+” additions

The following inventory compares Bacil F. Kirtley’s A Motif-Index of Traditional Polynesian Narratives (1971), in the supplied digital edition, with Thompson’s revised and enlarged Motif-Index (1955–58).

Scope: all 6,062 entry starts in Kirtley’s main index were screened. The comparison concerns the index numbers and descriptions, not an independent reading of every narrative cited by Kirtley. The working TMI reference is Katja Mellmann’s transcription of the revised edition, supplemented by Thompson’s text and targeted checks of the printed first volume. A claim of complete agreement with every page of all six printed TMI volumes would go beyond these checks.

Page numbers below are those printed in the supplied Kirtley PDF; add 36 to locate the corresponding PDF-viewer page. Kirtley’s descriptions are transcribed with line breaks removed and bibliographical references omitted. TMI descriptions are paraphrased unless individual words are quoted. Repeated labels are retained as separate entries.

As requested, list 1 contains unmarked entries only. Entries with “+” belong in list 3. Entries carrying both “*” and “+” appear in both lists 2 and 3 when their numerical code already exists in TMI.

RESULTS
- List 1: 11 substantive description mismatches, plus 10 explicitly qualified cases for review.
- List 2: 28 starred entry occurrences using 25 existing TMI heading numbers; one additional number, A939.1, survives as a cross-reference and is reported separately.
- List 3: 397 entries with a textual “+”, plus three entries with graphical cross-like marks whose interpretation as “+” remains provisional.

Kirtley expressly allows minor variants of Thompson’s motifs. Ordinary shortening, spelling differences, local names, and compatible examples are therefore not counted automatically as different motifs. Nor does an asterisk on an existing number necessarily imply a different meaning.

1. UNMARKED ENTRIES WITH SUBSTANTIVE DESCRIPTION MISMATCHES

These are discrepancies between the descriptions as written. Where a typographical error could explain the discrepancy, it is identified below; these should not all be described as deliberate reassignments.

1. A714.7 — p. 47
   Kirtley: Origin of sun: from right eye of god when he dies.
   TMI: Rama tears out his eyes following his brother’s death; they become the sun and moon.
   Comment: The death concerns a different person, and Kirtley specifies the right eye and the sun alone. This changes the event, beyond merely replacing a deity’s name.

2. A1335.3 — p. 80
   Kirtley: Origin of death from unsuccessful imitation of bad creator. The bad creator attempts in vain to endow his creations with life like the good creator. Fails and thus introduces death.
   TMI: People lose everlasting life through an unwise choice between two bundles.
   Comment: Kirtley repeats A1335.3 on this page. The other occurrence (unwise choice) agrees with TMI. The unsuccessful-imitation entry corresponds to TMI A1335.2.

3. A2775 — p. 112
   Kirtley: Why babies have soft spots in head.
   TMI: Explanation of why some trees are stunted.
   Comment: Human anatomy is assigned a number used by TMI for the stunted growth of trees.

4. C984.3 — p. 163
   Kirtley: Storm because of broken tabu.
   TMI: A broken taboo causes a flood.
   Comment: Kirtley repeats C984.3 on this page. The flood entry agrees with TMI; the storm entry corresponds to TMI C984.2.

5. D434.3 — p. 178
   Kirtley: Transformation: canoe-builder to person.
   TMI: A canoe-bailer becomes a person.
   Comment: Builder and bailer are different entities. This is a caption-level error candidate, probably a typographical substitution rather than a deliberately new classification; the shared Beckwith reference supports that possibility.

6. D491.3 — p. 181
   Kirtley: Magic dog shrinks in size.
   TMI: A saint confines a large volume of water in a small ditch.
   Comment: An animal shrinking and water being compressed into a ditch are different specific motifs.

7. D1882 — p. 217
   Kirtley: Rejuvenation of supernatural person.
   TMI: A supernatural being rejuvenates someone.
   Comment: The supernatural person changes from the agent of rejuvenation to its recipient. This is a caption-level role reversal; a one-word typographical error remains possible.

8. E235.2.2 — p. 242
   Kirtley: Ghost returns because corpse was not properly buried.
   TMI: A ghost returns because its corpse was improperly cremated.
   Comment: Burial replaces cremation. TMI E235.2 is the broader proper-burial category; E235.2.2 specifically concerns burning.

9. F420.5.2.2.2 — p. 293
   Kirtley: Water-spirits kidnap mortals and keep them under water.
   TMI: A mistreated boy calls on water spirits to take him; rising water engulfs him.
   Comment: Kirtley’s wording corresponds to TMI F420.5.2.2, the parent category. The extra final .2 assigns it to a different, more specific episode.

10. H1535 — p. 366
   Kirtley: Precipice test: jumping from cliff, survivor is winner.
   TMI: A hero is pushed over a precipice as a test.
   Comment: Kirtley describes a jumping contest; Thompson describes a hero being pushed over a precipice.

11. K98.1 — p. 379
   Kirtley: Cock fight won by deception.
   TMI: A beauty contest is won through deception involving leaves.
   Comment: The competition is changed from a beauty contest to a cockfight.

1A. ADDITIONAL UNMARKED CASES REQUIRING INTERPRETATION

These ten cases are included to make the review transparent, but are not counted among the eleven substantive mismatches above. They may be legitimate variants, imprecise wording or transcription errors.

1. A617.2 — p. 40
   Kirtley: Creation of earth from calabash.
   TMI: The universe is created from a calabash.
   Qualification: Earth is narrower than universe. This may be a local cosmological adaptation rather than a separate motif.

2. D1651.14 — p. 206
   Kirtley: Magic cock flies only at owner’s command.
   TMI: The digital reference says “clock” where Kirtley says “cock”.
   Qualification: Likely a transcription or printing problem in the reference: both point to Métraux, Ethnology, p.367. Do not count this as an established Kirtley misclassification without checking the printed D-volume.

3. E113 — p. 239
   Kirtley: Resuscitation from blood.
   TMI: Blood is used to restore life.
   Qualification: “From” may mean reconstruction from blood, whereas “by” describes a means of revival. The cited narratives must decide whether the distinction is real.

4. E607.2.2 — p. 255
   Kirtley: Rebirth by crawling into woman’s womb. (Idea present in Maui story, though hero fails at accomplishment.)
   TMI: Rebirth takes place by entering a woman’s womb.
   Qualification: The title agrees, but Kirtley explicitly says Maui fails. This is an acknowledged use for an attempted, unsuccessful version of the motif.

5. E783.2 — p. 267
   Kirtley: Severed head grows.
   TMI: A severed head grows back.
   Qualification: Growth in size and regeneration differ, but the missing “re-” may simply be a typographical or shorthand issue.

6. K831 — p. 391
   Kirtley: Victim killed by being bathed.
   TMI: The victim is killed during a bath.
   Qualification: Kirtley’s “by” makes bathing the means of killing; Thompson’s “while” only specifies the occasion. The source story must decide whether this is imprecision or a different mechanism.

7. K891.5.3 — p. 393
   Kirtley: Dupes deceived into falling over precipice.
   TMI: The victims are persuaded to let themselves be thrown over a precipice.
   Qualification: Falling through deception does not necessarily mean agreeing to be thrown. Kirtley may be broadening or loosely paraphrasing the same episode.

8. L50 — p. 412
   Kirtley: Victorious younger daughter.
   TMI: The youngest daughter triumphs.
   Qualification: Younger does not necessarily mean youngest. It may nevertheless describe the same person in the cited stories.

9. Q272 — p. 432
   Kirtley: Greed (gluttony) punished.
   TMI: Avarice is punished.
   Qualification: Gluttony concerns eating, whereas avarice concerns possessiveness or wealth. Kirtley signals his extension in parentheses, consistent with his stated practice for minor variants.

10. T145.0.1 — p. 459
   Kirtley: Polygamy.
   TMI: Polygyny: a man has multiple wives.
   Qualification: Polygamy includes polygyny and polyandry; polygyny is narrower. The citations may all concern polygyny, in which case this is terminological broadening.
