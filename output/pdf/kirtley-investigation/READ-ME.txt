EXTRACTION AND SCREENING LIMITS
Poppler pdftotext 26.09.0 was used, in layout and bounding-box layout modes.
The PDF has a text layer; no blanket OCR was applied. Text alone is insufficient:
visual marks beside J2048 on printed p.375 are absent from extracted text and
remain visible when PDF annotations are hidden. Their provenance is unresolved.

The candidate screen considered entry-like labels at the left margin in the
main index, excluding range headings and front-matter synopsis. It allowed
asterisk/plus markers, internal spaces after decimal points, and trailing
commas as well as periods. It does not establish completeness of segmentation,
resolve every malformed code, or distinguish all inherited headings and
cross-references. No descriptions or citations were silently corrected.
The 82 repeated labels are groups, not a count of erroneous entries.

Visual inspection covered physical PDF pages 8, 40, 41, 42, 116, 199, 269,
270, 349, 358, 411 and 415. This is targeted verification, not a visual review
of all 522 pages. Printed pages, continuations, markers and duplicate wording
were checked on the relevant rendered pages. Final printed page 486 was also
checked by text extraction.

RECOMMENDED INVESTIGATION FORMAT
Keep two layers: immutable source evidence and a reviewable structured corpus.
Use linked UTF-8 CSV tables, optionally presented as workbook tabs. The corpus
should be independent of application story-import CSVs: one index entry is not
one story, and one bibliographic reference is not necessarily one narrative.

1. Entry occurrences
   One row per actual occurrence in Kirtley, even when labels repeat.
   Stable occurrence ID independent of the trope code; document identity;
   physical PDF page and printed page; entry order; source coordinates and
   text line span; raw label and raw complete text; transcribed caption and
   descriptive continuation; literal '*'/'+' marks; uncertainty about graphic
   marks; normalized code stored separately; surrounding heading and explicit
   cross-references; transcription/review status.
   Example proposed ID: KIRTLEY1971-p379-e02. Do not use K98.1 as the row key.

2. Bibliographic works
   Full bibliography entries exactly as given; stable work IDs; author and
   year as transcribed; proposed resolved author, title and edition separately;
   unresolved abbreviations and identity questions explicitly recorded.

3. Citation occurrences
   One row per entry-to-source citation. Preserve geographic labels, raw
   reference, volume, page/number/note locators, bibliography link and inherited
   context. Preserve ambiguous joint places and grouped citations. Do not
   assume a citation uniquely identifies a story. Do not split mechanically
   on every comma or semicolon without checking reference structure.

4. TMI reference records
   Separate namespace and edition identity, exact source text, reference
   snapshot and locator. Preserve first-edition links and editorial corrections
   as separate information. Resolve cross-reference-only omissions from a
   suitable printed/digital witness before declaring a code absent.

5. Alignment assertions
   Link each Kirtley occurrence to zero, one or several TMI records. Record
   exact equivalence, narrower meaning, broader meaning, partial overlap,
   incompatible meaning, proposed new trope, or unresolved alignment. Keep
   Kirtley's stated classification separate from the investigator's conclusion.
   Same number is a candidate link, not proof of equivalence.

6. Issues and decisions
   Issue ID; affected occurrences; issue type; evidence from both witnesses;
   suggested correction or alternative links; confidence; reviewer; decision
   and date. Allow multiple issues per entry. Preserve earlier decisions and
   source wording. Never renumber the historical source to conceal a conflict.

KIRTLEY'S OWN CONVENTIONS
The introduction, printed p.viii / PDF p.8, distinguishes inherited entries,
parenthetically qualified variants, starred proposed additions with plausible
numbering, and '+' additions whose placement he considered uncertain.
Repeated '+' placements therefore require interpretation, not automatic
rejection. Asterisks before bibliographic authorities are a different context
from asterisks attached to entry labels. Preserve their position.

AUDIT ORDER AND COMPLETION CRITERIA
A. Validate transcription and segmentation across every page. Assign IDs,
   preserve continuations and markers, and reconcile totals by chapter/page.
B. Resolve structural issues: repeated labels, syntax, order, hierarchy,
   reference existence, edition differences and new-code collisions.
C. Compare all unmarked and qualified entries against the correct TMI wording;
   compare additions with their proposed parent and possible equivalents
   elsewhere in TMI. Check both meaning and classification granularity.
D. Review all citations against the bibliography for identity, years, volumes,
   locations and locators. Consult the underlying narratives to establish
   whether they actually support Kirtley's assigned trope. Catalogue comparison
   alone cannot establish that narrative-level claim.
E. Record cultural/translation mismatches separately from numerical mistakes.
   Kirtley himself discusses the difficulty of translating Polynesian categories
   into Thompson's scheme on printed p.ix. TMI agreement is not the sole test
   of an analytically sound classification.
F. Publish a coverage ledger: every occurrence has an extraction status, an
   alignment status and a citation-review status; every unresolved issue stays
   visible. Report reviewed/total counts for each pass. Do not claim that a
   candidate screen has checked all incoherences.
